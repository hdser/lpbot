Decide every look by the steps below, in order, and stop at the first one that gives an action. Take the figures from the look's table: C90 and C30 are the `change` of the 90d and 30d lines, V30 and V1 the `vol/day` of the 30d and 1d lines.

Step 1. Is the long timeframe against you? It is when C90 is {beyond_zero}, or C30 is {beyond_zero} by more than 3 x V30. Nothing else makes it so: a `change` compares the price now with one price 30 or 90 days ago, so C30 crossing zero within a day is a day's move, and so is one new 30-day high or low.

Step 2. With a position open:
- a. The long timeframe is against you (step 1): `exit`.
- b. The position is `resting`, wholly in {value}, and has earned fees, so price went into the range and came out again: `exit`. The round trip is done.
- c. Anything else: `hold`. A range that price is inside or has gone through has traded {value} for the other token, as it was placed to do; closing it there swaps back at a worse price than it traded at. A resting range that has earned nothing yet stays where it is: do not move it after the price.

Step 3. With no position:
- a. The long timeframe is against you (step 1): `stay_out`.
- b. Neither the 4h nor the 1d line shows {entry_move}, or the 15m and 1h lines show it still running: `stay_out`, and look again within a few hours.
- c. Otherwise `enter` a range {one_sided}: its near edge next to the price, its width at least 3 x V1.

In every `--reasoning`, write C90, C30 and 3 x V30, then the step that decided, such as `2c`, and what it saw.
