Long timeframe first, then the short one. Read every look from the bottom of the table up.

1. The 30- and 90-day lines are the trend: how far price moved over them and where it sits between their highs and lows. Your ranges sit {one_sided}, so they are paid when the long timeframe is {favoured}, and left holding the wrong token when it is {against}. While the 30- and 90-day lines are clearly {against}, stay out.
2. The day and week lines say whether price is going back and forth or running. Prefer a market that goes back and forth inside the long trend.
3. The 15-minute to 4-hour lines only time the entry: enter after {entry_move} against the long trend has slowed, not while it runs. A range next to the price is filled by an ordinary day: make it at least three times the day line's `vol/day` wide.

What clearly {against} means. A long line's `change` compares the price now with one price 30 or 90 days ago, so a single day's move shifts it by as much as that day moved: a 30-day change that crosses zero within a day is a day's move, not a turn, and so is one new 30-day high or low. The long timeframe is clearly {against} only when the 90-day change is {beyond_zero}, or the 30-day change is {beyond_zero} by more than three times the 30-day line's `vol/day`. Anything less is a pullback inside the trend, which is what your range is placed for.

With a position open:
- A range that price has moved into or through has traded {value} for the other token, as it was placed to do. Closing it there swaps back at a worse price than it traded at and leaves you to enter again after the price has come back. Hold it through the pullback. Give it up only when the long timeframe is clearly {against} by the test above, never for an hour's or a day's move; the run's own limits stop a loss that grows.
- Exit when it has made its round trip: back wholly in {value} with its fees, after price went into the range and came out again.
- Do not move a resting range after the price. Left behind, it costs nothing; a new range needs {entry_move} first.

In every `--reasoning`, say what the long, the middle and the short timeframe each told you, and for the long one give the two figures of the test.
